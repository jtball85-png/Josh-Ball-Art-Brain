question after "Steve" (bodysurfer/wave, B&W) became the first real photo
to clear the print-derivation pipeline.

## Context

## FinerWorks' own advice for photographers

Per FinerWorks' support article ("Quick Paper Advice for Photographers"):
- Glossy/baryta gives images more "pop" (deeper darks, brighter lights) but
  can show glare under certain light.
- Matte avoids glare/shine, better for framed display under mixed lighting.
- Smaller prints (≤11x14) benefit from a smooth/low-texture surface to
  preserve fine detail; larger prints can carry more texture.
- Cotton-rag papers without unstable optical brighteners hold their color
  and whites longer than wood-pulp papers.

## Matching the CEO's home Canon papers

- **Canon glossy → Hahnemühle Photo Rag Baryta.** Baryta is the fine-art
  equivalent of a glossy photo paper, but with a fiber-based darkroom look
  instead of a plasticky sheen — no glare, deeper blacks.
- **Canon matte → Hahnemühle Photo Rag.** FinerWorks' flagship matte,
  closest in hand-feel and look to Canon's Photo Paper Pro Matte.

## Recommendation

For "Steve" specifically — a high-contrast B&W wave/spray shot where
tonal range in the whitewater and foam detail matters — sample:

1. **Photo Rag Baryta** (glossy-family candidate, and the traditional
   choice for serious black & white printing)
2. **Photo Rag** (matte-family candidate, safe fine-art default)

This is the same two-paper spread the phase0 research already called for,
now anchored to a real photo instead of a hypothetical. If a third,
"most photographers pick this" option is wanted for comparison, add
**Moab Exhibition Luster 300** — a satin finish that splits the difference
and is the one FinerWorks itself cites as the pro favorite.
